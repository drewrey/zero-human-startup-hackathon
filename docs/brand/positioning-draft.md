## Proposed promise

**"Spot it. Say it. Know whether to buy it."**

- **One-sentence pitch:** A voice-first sourcing assistant for part-time apparel resellers: tell it
  what you found at the thrift store, then hear a buying recommendation and check the sold comps
  behind it.
- **Short explanation:** Say the brand, item, and tag price. Answer up to two quick questions if a
  detail matters. Hear the recommendation; glance at the phone to see the comps and assumptions.

These are proposed copy, not tested customer language.

## What leads, what supports

1. **Hook: hands-free speed.** Describe an item out loud while still at the rack. Voice is the
   distinctive entry point.
2. **Payoff: a buying decision.** Hear BUY, MAYBE, or PASS, not just a list of prices, and see the
   assumptions and comps before trusting it.
3. **Expansion: more markets later.** Cross-marketplace comparison strengthens the promise once
   coverage is real; it is not a lead claim while Phase 1 has eBay sold comps only.

## Audience and distinction

First audience: side-hustle apparel resellers sourcing with their hands busy, who need an answer
before spending at the rack (the spec's primary persona). Position against a behavior, not an
unproven competitor claim: instead of leaving the rack to hunt sold listings by hand, talk through
the find and inspect the evidence in one flow. This is a hypothesis to test with users.

## Guardrails while it's a draft

- Don't promise all four marketplaces until live coverage exists (Phase 1: eBay sold comps).
- Don't promise exact after-fees profit; profit is an estimate with stated fee and shipping
  assumptions (BR-2/BR-3/BR-5).
- Don't claim "instant," "accurate," or end-to-end "hands-free" until the phone flow is tested;
  "voice-first" accurately describes the intended input.

## How to decide whether it works

Show one screen and one spoken demo to a few target resellers. Ask, unprompted: "What does this do?",
"When would you use it?", and "What would you need to trust its recommendation?" Record their
language and confusion before locking a headline.
